First Films at Meta House

Notes on the short films Anti‑Archive brought to Meta House this August — and the pattern that runs through them: mostly first films, mostly about the gap between what family expects and what you actually want.

Researched and written after the fact, from public sources. 7 min read · August 2026
ProgrammeAnti‑Archive Short Film Night
VenueMeta House · #48, St. 228
DateThu 20 Aug 2026
Time7:30 PM

Admit One

The programme was gone by the time we sat down to write this. That's on purpose. paperback isn't in the business of telling you what's on tonight — by the time you're reading this, the projector at Meta House is off and the six short films that filled Street 228 for one Thursday evening are back on hard drives, waiting for the next festival to say yes. What's left is the more interesting question: what does a single night of Cambodian short film, chosen by the people who help make it, actually add up to?

Anti‑Archive was founded in Phnom Penh in 2014 by the filmmakers Davy Chou, Steve Chen, and Kavich Neang — joined later as partners by Daniel Mattes and Park Sungho — specifically because that structure didn't exist yet. Cambodia's film industry had spent decades being rebuilt from almost nothing after the Khmer Rouge years gutted it, and there was no reliable production company for a new generation of Cambodian directors to make a first film through. Anti‑Archive produces and co-produces fiction and documentary work, mostly by first-time Cambodian filmmakers, alongside international productions shot in the region. The name is a small provocation: an archive is a record of what someone decided was worth keeping. Anti‑Archive is more interested in who gets to make that decision next.

The programme

5 of 6 verified

Titles, directors and details below are drawn from official film and festival pages, not the event listing alone — see Sources. One title on the night's programme couldn't be independently confirmed before publishing; it's noted, not described, at the end of this list.

01
Three Wheels Kavich Neang · 2015 · 20 min

A tuk-tuk driver spends his nights carrying the city's evenings around while an old memory keeps surfacing — a marriage from the Khmer Rouge years that was never his to choose. One of Neang's first significant shorts, made before the features that later took him to Rotterdam and Venice; it premiered at Busan and won the Youth Jury Award in Singapore.

02
California Dreaming Sreylin Meas · 2019 · 16 min

Two women from different worlds meet at a resort on the coast and find, briefly, a way out of their own lives. Meas's directorial debut, made through Anti‑Archive's Echoes from Tomorrow programme for first-time Cambodian filmmakers.

03
Happy Ending Élodie Yung · 2023

A Cambodian woman carrying a secret takes work in an illicit massage parlour in the United States, trying to build something better for the family she left behind. Better known as an actor — Marvel's Elektra, among other roles — the French-Cambodian Yung directs here for the first time.

04
Fresh as Daisy Socheata Van · 2025 · 13 min

Two young women raised in conservative households in the same Phnom Penh neighbourhood hold onto their friendship as they try to choose their own lives. Van's first fiction film, developed at the Purin Pictures Short Film Camp; it premiered at Singapore International Film Festival 2025, where it was also nominated for Best Performance.

05
Grandma Nai Who Played Favorites Chheangkea · 2025 · 19 min

On Tomb Sweeping Day, a dead grandmother sneaks out of a peaceful afterlife after overhearing that her queer grandson is about to get engaged. Chheangkea's NYU thesis film premiered at Sundance 2025, where it won a Short Film Award — a deadpan comedy built over a real question about who gets welcomed into a family's account of itself, and who doesn't.

A sixth title, Parade, credited to Davy Chou, was also listed on the evening's programme. We could not independently confirm its production details before publishing, so it isn't described above.

Four of these five are first films — a director's first fiction short, or first film, full stop. The fifth was made early, too.

That's not really a coincidence; it's close to the job Anti‑Archive gave itself in 2014. What's less obviously by design is the shape underneath the plots. A queer grandson waiting to find out if his family will show up. Two women who owe their households more compliance than they're willing to give. A woman keeping a secret from the family she's providing for. A marriage nobody in it chose. On this particular Thursday, Cambodian cinema kept circling the same fault line — inherited obligation on one side, a person's own account of their life on the other — and mostly declined to resolve it either way.

The venue is part of that story too. Meta House opened in 2007 in a private villa on Street 228, founded by the German filmmaker and arts activist Nico Mesterharm; it now runs a gallery, an open-air stage, a small library and a screening room alongside a Goethe-Institut language school. For almost two decades it has done the unglamorous work of giving Cambodian artists a room to show work in, at a point when the country's contemporary art and film scene was rebuilding itself nearly from zero. A short film night here isn't really an event in the usual sense. It's closer to routine maintenance on a piece of infrastructure the city still needs.

None of the five films above resolve their opening question. That might be the most honest way to end a programme about deciding what's worth keeping.
