The Riverside,
Before the Heat Arrives

Written while walking, three mornings running. 6 min read · Updated this month

For a few hours after sunrise, the strip along the Tonle Sap still belongs to the city itself — before the tour groups, before the heat, before the river turns the colour of old brass. Noodle carts open without ceremony. An old man moves through tai chi forms in the shadow of a colonial facade. The light comes low and sideways off the water, and for a while, everything looks like it’s being remembered rather than lived.

We have walked this stretch enough times now to know it by its sounds before its sights: the scrape of a cart being wheeled into position, the particular hiss of a first espresso shot, a monk’s sandals on wet pavement. Riverside is the oldest part of paperback’s index, and it is the one we return to most — not because it has changed the least, but because it rewards the specific hour you choose to see it.

Three addresses hold this morning together. The first is a doorway easy to miss — a espresso counter set behind a shopfront window that hasn’t changed its sign in sixty years, run by a family who has never once rushed an order. It opens at six. By seven, the four stools along the counter are usually taken by the same regulars, reading the same newspapers, in the same order.

“We don’t open for tourists. We open because the street opens.” Shopkeeper, Street 178, in passing conversation

A few doors down, a secondhand bookshop keeps hours nobody has ever successfully explained to us. Paperbacks are stacked less by genre than by whim — a Graham Greene beside a Khmer-English dictionary beside a water-stained atlas. The owner reads more than he sells, and seems entirely at peace with that arrangement.

By late morning the light changes register entirely. The low gold gives way to a flatter, whiter glare, and the street’s morning cast — the tai chi practitioners, the newspaper regulars, the cart vendors — disperses into the rest of the day. This is usually where we stop, too. Not because there is nothing left to see, but because the specific thing we came for has already happened.

We came back that evening, mostly out of curiosity. Riverside after dark is a different notebook entry entirely — amber light, a long bar counter facing the water, a menu that doesn’t open until the sun is fully down. Worth its own field note, another time.

For now: come early, come slowly, and don’t mistake the quiet hour for an empty one.
